# Animal Room Instagram playbook

This is a small, sustainable system for using `@thebioactivekeeper` to help
people discover Animal Room without turning the account into an advertisement.
The animals and the real room stay at the center. The software appears when it
helps tell that story.

## Profile and link

Use `https://animalroom.app/` as the first profile link. Once the public demo is
live, use `https://animalroom.app/demo/` for launch posts that ask people to try
the apps before installing them.

Suggested bio copy:

> Bioactive builds, reptiles & the software behind our animal room.<br>
> Bask + Shed + Haven · free, local-first and open source.

Suggested Story highlights:

- **The room** — enclosure tours and the wall display in daily use
- **Bask** — sensors, alerts, lighting cycles and climate trends
- **Shed** — care tasks, weights, feeding and family workflows
- **The build** — Raspberry Pi, hardware, failures and fixes
- **Support** — what tips funded and the next compatibility goal

## Three pinned posts

### 1. Why this exists

Open with a real wide photograph or short walk-through of the animal room.
Explain the original problem: many enclosures, multiple keepers, and no calm
way to know both whether the habitats were safe and whether today's care was
finished. End by showing the Haven display in the same room.

### 2. Bask in one minute

Show a physical sensor, the live reading, and the wall dashboard. Include one
honest limitation and say that monitoring is local and passive. Link to the
interactive demo rather than asking viewers to install immediately.

### 3. Shed and shared care

Record one real care task from the phone, then show it disappear from the room
display and appear in history. Do not show a keeper name, access code, local IP,
private note, or household reward. End with “free and open source — link in
bio.”

## A low-effort monthly rhythm

Keep ordinary animal posts ordinary. Among project-related posts, rotate these
four formats; there is no need to publish all four every month.

1. **Real workflow:** morning room check, misting round, weighing day, feeding
   preparation, or a lighting change.
2. **One feature:** show one action and one result in 15–30 seconds.
3. **Build note:** a bug, hardware choice, failed idea, security improvement, or
   compatibility test and what changed because of it.
4. **Keeper question:** ask one concrete question that could improve the app,
   then report back later on what was changed.

Use the privacy-safe share card from Bask or Shed as the last frame of a
carousel or Reel. Pair it with one real photograph or phone recording so the
post cannot be mistaken for a synthetic product advertisement.

## Recording recipe

For a short Reel:

1. **Problem, 2–3 seconds:** “With this many enclosures, I needed one place to
   see what actually needs attention.”
2. **Physical context, 4–6 seconds:** show the animal room, sensor, feeder bin,
   or care task.
3. **One action, 8–15 seconds:** open the app and perform one real workflow.
4. **Result, 4–6 seconds:** show the wall display or privacy-safe share card.
5. **One invitation:** “It is free, local-first, and open source. Try the demo
   from the link in my bio.”

Avoid rapid screen recordings, tiny text, fake phone frames, long feature
lists, and AI-generated lifestyle art. Natural room sound or a plain spoken
explanation is enough.

## Reusable captions

### Project introduction

> I built these because this is the room they have to work in every day. Bask
> watches enclosure temperature and humidity. Shed keeps our household care
> list and history. Haven puts the two together on the room display. They run
> on our own Raspberry Pi, keep the data here, and are free and open source.
> You can try the demo before installing anything — link in bio.

### Feature post

> A small thing that removes a surprising amount of guesswork: [describe the
> one feature]. This is running against our real animal-room data; the share
> card hides household and device details before it leaves the app. What would
> make this workflow more useful in your room?

### Build or testing post

> This week the unglamorous work was [specific bug, test, security change, or
> compatibility problem]. The fix was [plain-language outcome]. The code,
> tests, limitations and install script are public, because local-first only
> means something if people can inspect how it works.

### Occasional support post

> Bask, Shed and Haven are staying free and open source. If they save you time
> or help your animals, optional tips help cover test sensors, Raspberry Pi
> hardware, the domain and compatibility work. The current goal is [specific
> device or test]. Support Animal Room through the link in bio — sharing a
> setup or filing a useful bug report helps too.

Use the support caption no more than once for every three or four useful
project posts. Never attach a tip request to an animal health event, emergency,
or husbandry warning.

## Trust and privacy checklist

Before posting any app screen or recording:

- Hide local IP addresses, access/setup codes, tokens and device identifiers.
- Hide household names, keeper attribution, rewards and private notes.
- Prefer the app's redacted share mode. Include animal names only deliberately.
- Do not frame schedules or target ranges as universal care advice.
- Do not imply affiliation with hardware makers or husbandry resources.
- Say “AI-assisted, human-reviewed and tested” when the build process is
  relevant; do not turn every animal post into a defense of the toolchain.
- If someone reports a problem, ask for a reproducible case or a private
  security report instead of debating a vague accusation in comments.

## Turning attention into support

Keep the public path consistent:

**Support Animal Room → `https://animalroom.app/support/` → Ko-fi**

The support page explains what tips fund and includes ways to help without
paying. GitHub's native Sponsor button may still link directly to Ko-fi.

On Ko-fi, use one visible goal tied to a real, priced piece of work. A good
default is “Next compatibility device”: name the requested sensor or controller,
set the target to its actual purchase and shipping cost, and promise only to
buy it, test it in the real room, and publish the result. Post a short follow-up
when a goal is funded so supporters can see what happened.

Do not promise feature priority, private husbandry advice, or safety features in
exchange for support. The software and animal-safety functionality stay open to
everyone.

## What to watch

Review once a month rather than after every post:

- Visits to the demo and install guide
- GitHub stars, issues and setup stories
- New people sharing their own setup
- Successful outside installations and the questions they generated
- Tips per project-related post, not follower count alone

No application telemetry is needed. Direct replies, GitHub activity and Ko-fi
support are enough to learn whether the right people are finding the project.
